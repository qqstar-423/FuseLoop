## Performance Evidence Basis

Performance data follows the cann-bench reports. Stage6 selects `kernel_details`; baseline, HAP, and scoring are the tool's responsibility, with no independent baseline re-measurement; this basis aggregates kernel execution time and excludes inter-core synchronization or scheduling gaps. Historical `trace_view` scores cannot be compared directly with this round, nor used to judge up/down.

The program stores this measurement's hardware, task, and evaluation protocol in `eval/iter<iter>/perf_result.json`, and stores whether the two rounds are comparable and why in `eval/iter<iter>/perf_comparison.json`. Follow that conclusion first: with different bases, old reports lacking a basis, incomplete cases, or a different benchmark, do not infer up/down from the two rounds' average difference, and do not fill in this round's success/regression experience. Complete new results may serve as a new baseline; do not derive "improvement/regression" yourself from old history numbers, and do not treat the current environment's basis as the old reports' basis.

The formal summary is in `eval/<iter>/perf_result.json`; trace the original reports and cases via its `source_json` and `kernel_csv`. Combine code and profiler to verify the level where intermediate data resides, the movement volume, and the overhead; do not substitute kernel count shares or zero HBM write-back for performance evidence. If consecutive calls expose stale data caching, fix correctness first; do not use previously passing cases to prove the current implementation is effective.
